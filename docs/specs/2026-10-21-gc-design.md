# Garbage collection: finished sessions clean up after themselves - design

Date: 2026-10-21. Status: first version, for the person's review. Agreed with the person part by part in a Claude
Code session; built on two read-only analyses of the twosuns field repository made the same day (a git
specialist's and a software engineer's, run as separate agents). Marked **(session's call)** where the session
decided and the person has not confirmed. Everything else is the person's decision, quoted or restated.

## 1. What it is for

A fleet leaves its trees and branches behind. Decision 206 (2026-10-02) made `retire` and `fleet down` remove a
seat's tree and branch when the work is surely on trunk, and `flotilla fleet clean` sweeps what forced stops left.
Three weeks later the pile is back, and the sweep barely touches it.

**Measured on twosuns, 2026-10-21** (91 worktrees, 16.2 GB, the disk 90 % full; it filled twice that week):

- `fleet clean` would remove 7 trees and keep 83 (31 for ignored data, 37 "not on trunk", 9 dirty, 24 with
  commits only a reflog reaches; reasons overlap).
- The 70 trees whose HEAD is not on `main` are detached checkouts of long-lived lines (`proto/teaser-*`, 400 to 1100
  commits ahead of `main`). That work never reaches `main` by design: its trunk in practice is the local branch
  `proto/teaser-v4`. But 88 of the 90 trees have their HEAD on another ref, so removing the tree loses no commit -
  the branch keeps them. The sweep asks "is it on trunk", which is the branch's question, and asks it of the tree.
- 85 of 90 trees are detached because `retire` already ran on them: it lets go of the branch and leaves the tree
  when the judge keeps it. Retire did its job; the judge's terms are what keep the trees.
- 36 twosuns sessions in the census are dead (`pid: null`). A session that dies never runs `retire`, so nothing
  judges its tree until someone runs `fleet clean` by hand.
- Ignored data holds 32 trees: renders brought back from the GPU machine (`teaser-out*`, 4.4 GB, 105 mp4 and 1437
  png), the person's 3D models sent to it (`*3d-in`, 1.3 GB), `gpu-checks-out`. Much of it is byte-identical to files
  in the project's feedback folder (`/home/max/twosuns-feedback-1`), where sessions hand results to the person:
  all 23 copies of `spire3d-in/` (one 23.3 MB set) are twins of
  `twosuns-feedback-1/teaser-spire/model-2-work/spire3d-in/`. 3.48 GB in 1867 files has no twin anywhere.
- A bug: `main-50/tools/crystal3d/` holds only `__pycache__`, but `git status --ignored=traditional` folds it into
  one line `tools/crystal3d/`, and `_regenerable` reads that name as somebody's data.
- 7 shell loops whose session is gone (parent pid 1, 11 to 17 days old) sit on the machine; two of them in trees.

Nothing in twosuns was ever squash-merged; all 20 `batch/*` branches were fast-forwarded with their merges intact,
and the ledger never contradicts git (126 shipped tips, 127 recorded merges, 0 disagreements). Better landing
detection changes nothing here: the gain is in judging the tree by what it alone holds.

**The person's scenario.** The person comes back to twosuns after a week. The fleet has run three features; two
sessions died overnight, the orchestrator released four. Today they find 91 trees and a full disk. With the
collector they find the trees of finished sessions gone, and one message from the orchestrator: "cleaned 63 trees
and 331 branches; moved 640 MB of renders with no copy anywhere to `twosuns-gc-archive/` (list attached, `flotilla
gc restore` brings any back); three trees wait for you - `main-25` holds 373 untracked files". Nothing they made is
lost, and the one thing worth a look is in front of them.

## 2. The rule: two questions before anything goes

The person's decision: before removing anything - a tree, a branch, a file, an artefact - the collector answers two
questions, and the answers decide what it does.

1. **Does anything break if it goes?** A live session's work; a branch another tree stands on or a batch being
   built from; an open ledger row that names it; a rig job still bringing results back into the tree.
2. **Can it be brought back if the removal was a mistake?** A copy exists elsewhere (a ref, origin, a byte-identical
   file in a place that survives); a command makes it again (`npm install`, a committed script); it was moved to the
   archive; or nothing brings it back.

| breaks something? | can it come back? | what the collector does |
|---|---|---|
| no | yes - a copy or a rebuild | removes it itself; one line in the report |
| no | only from the archive | moves it to the archive; small: tells the person after; large: asks before |
| yes | - | leaves it, and names whose work would break |
| - | nothing brings it back | asks, always |

The person's words for the third and fourth rows: untracked files, files another AI may have made, files git
ignores and nothing makes again, and anything unclear are clarified with the person, never decided.

**Small or large** (second row) is weighed on five signals the person accepted, of which the third matters most:

1. **Size** - a threshold per pass (session's call: 100 MB moved in one pass is large; the profile may change it).
2. **Kind** - media (video, renders, audio, images) is heavy and costly to make again; scripts, logs and json weigh
   megabytes.
3. **Can it be made again** - a committed script or manifest that produces it (a lockfile beside `node_modules`,
   a job script whose output directory it is). The person: "especially important".
4. **Has the person seen it** - a copy sits in a place the project declares as the person's (the feedback folder).
5. **Freshness** - yesterday's render is worth more than one from two weeks ago.

How they combine (session's call). Signals 3 and 4 mostly act before this point: a file a committed script makes
again, or with a twin in a declared store, can come back and is tier 1 (section 6). What reaches tier 2 is decided
by size: the files one pass would archive from one tree, summed - over the threshold, the person is asked before;
under it, told after. Kind and freshness do not move the line; they order the list and the question ("4 mp4 from
yesterday, 380 MB") so the person can answer at a glance. A file whose regeneration a committed script names but
cannot run here (a rig job) is still tier 2: it can be made again only at a price.

## 3. Three tiers (the person's)

1. **Plainly garbage: the session removes it itself**, on any of the occasions in section 4. Rows 1 and 3 of the
   table: it can come back, nothing breaks.
2. **Not clear it may go.** Small: archive it and tell the person. Large: ask. The five signals decide which.
3. **Needs the person.** Untracked files, other AIs' files, uncommitted edits, anything the collector cannot
   classify. The question shows the evidence (for example "byte-identical to `b1aba0df` on origin") so the answer is
   quick, but the collector does not answer it. The answers it offers (session's call): **archive and remove** (the
   files go to the archive, the tree goes; restorable for the archive's month), **keep** (the tree stays; asked again
   only when something in it changes or the disk runs low), or **the person's words** (for example "commit these to
   the branch first"), carried out by a session as ordinary work. No answer deletes for good: the only permanent
   deletion in the collector is the archive's expiry (section 7), so everything removed stays recoverable for a
   month.

## 4. When it runs

The person's occasions, each a moment when a session's own work is done and merged:

- a. the fleet is stood down, the work done and merged;
- b. a feature is done and merged, and the session goes on to a new one (its finished branch and anything the old
  task left in the tree);
- c. the orchestrator releases a session, its work merged and done;
- d. sessions stood down while they wait.

Added by the session from flotilla's lifecycle (session's call; the person asked for more occasions to be checked):

- e. **A session died without cleaning up.** The commonest case in twosuns. Whoever notices - the orchestrator,
  `flotilla doctor`, the daily pass - judges the dead seat's tree as `retire` would.
- f. **The sender shipped a batch** (`shipped`). Many branches become provably landed at once; one pass over them.
- g. **A helper finished** (`helper done`): its seat is released and its tree left behind.
- h. **Work was released** (`released`: decided it will not happen). Not tier 1: its branch is not landed. It is a
  reason to ask (tier 2 or 3) whether to park and archive it.
- i. **Work was adopted** (`adopt`): the tree changes owner and is not judged. A guard, not an occasion.
- j. **The disk runs low** (section 8).
- k. **A daily pass** as the net under all of the above (`fleet clean` on a schedule the orchestrator keeps).

## 5. What the collector judges, and how

### 5.1 A tree and its branch are two decisions

A tree goes when it holds nothing that exists nowhere else; its branch is judged on its own (5.3). This replaces
decision 206's single test for the tree. A tree may go while its branch stays.

Per tree, in order (adapted from the git specialist's algorithm; the trees judged are the fleet's own, as today -
a person's own tree is never judged):

1. **Alive?** An open row holds the tree for a session the census lists (decision 208), or a lock flotilla did not
   write, or one naming a live session (decision 209) - keep, named. The process's working directory is not
   liveness: every seat runs from the main checkout (decision 208), and pid-1 orphans are leftovers, not sessions.
2. **In use right now?** A process with its working directory inside the tree, or `index.lock` present - keep,
   named; `git worktree remove` ignores `index.lock` (verified on git 2.53), so the collector checks it. Orphans the
   way `leftovers.py` finds them are stopped first, as `retire` does.
3. **Clean?** Status with its options spelled out (decision 207, C1); no skip-worktree or assume-unchanged files.
   Anything uncommitted or untracked is tier 3.
4. **Commits held elsewhere?** HEAD is contained in a ref that survives the pass (`for-each-ref --contains` over
   heads, remotes and tags; other trees' HEADs do not count, since they may go in the same pass). The tree's own
   branch counts when it stays: a clean tree whose session died on an unlanded branch is tier 1 - the tree goes, the
   branch keeps the commits, and a tree can be cut from it again (session's call). When no surviving ref holds HEAD,
   the tree is the sole holder (`judge-5`, `judge-6` today): tier 3. Commits only the tree's reflog reaches are
   **parked** (5.4) and the tree goes on.
5. **Ignored data safe?** Section 6.
6. **Remove** (5.5).

### 5.2 What "landed" means for a branch

Unchanged from decisions 206 and 207: every commit on origin's trunk as origin answers at that moment, or a squash
whose exact tip the ledger shipped. The person's rule "commits that exist nowhere else are never lost" is read by
content for a branch (a squash-shipped tip has landed though its objects are not on trunk) and by reachability for a
tree (session's call; the two readings agree on everything twosuns holds).

In local flow (the default: "work stays on this machine; nothing is pushed") the profile's trunk is the trunk and
there is no origin to ask; a branch whose commits are all on the local trunk has landed (session's call - otherwise
the collector would never delete a branch in a default project).

The project may name more than one trunk (session's call): a long-lived integration line like `proto/teaser-v4`
is a trunk for the branches cut from it, so a seat branch merged into it is landed there and is deleted - its
commits stay on that trunk, so nothing breaks and they can come back (the two questions of section 2). A trunk
that exists only on this machine is a risk the report names ("`proto/teaser-v4`: 109 commits only on this disk"),
not a reason to keep the branches merged into it.

### 5.3 Branches

- **Local branches** are deleted on decision 206's terms. The set judged widens to the branches the sender makes
  (in twosuns `batch/*`, `ship/*`, `sender-<n>/*`, `preview/*`): today 96 are kept as "flotilla did not make it",
  89 of them ancestors of trunk. No flotilla code names them - the sender sessions chose the names - so a name match
  could catch a person's own branch, which decision 207 (I5) never judges. From now on flotilla records the branches
  a sender creates (session's call: a ledger record, so the judged set is known, not guessed). Branches made before
  that are not judged by name: the first pass lists landed branches whose maker it cannot tell as one tier 2
  question ("89 landed branches nobody recorded: `batch/*` 20, `ship/*` 45, ... delete?").
- **Branches on origin** are never deleted by the collector (session's call): a remote branch may be another
  clone's work, and the collector cannot see other clones. Remote branches already landed are listed for the person
  as a tier 2 question.
- A branch is deleted only while it still points at the judged commit (decision 207, M4), as today.

### 5.4 Parking

A commit that would become unreachable - reflog-only, or the HEAD of a released task the person chose to keep - is
pinned as `refs/flotilla/parked/<tree>/<sha>` before the tree goes. Git would otherwise expire it silently after 30
days. Parking makes such a commit recoverable, so a tree held only by reflog-only commits is tier 1 (in twosuns
those commits are throwaway preview and trial merges: 0 of 134 sampled patches are on `main`). Parked refs live
and expire with the archive (section 7).

### 5.5 Removing, and racing a session

`git worktree remove` deletes ignored files without a word and does not check that HEAD stays reachable (both
verified on git 2.53), so it is the last check only for what it does check. The removal runs inside the ledger's
lock (the one `tree.py` cuts trees under), takes a `git worktree lock` as a mutex (a spawn's lock wins), re-reads
HEAD, status, `index.lock` and `/proc` under it, then unlocks and removes without `--force`. A HEAD that moved since
the judgement aborts the removal. A removal git refuses still frees the seat's branch and says why (decision 207,
I6).

## 6. Ignored files: by content, not by name

1. **Regenerable** stays a name list (`REGENERABLE`), applied to the entry itself (decision 207, I1), with two
   changes. A folded directory (`tools/crystal3d/`) is judged by its contents: it is regenerable when every file in
   it is. And evidence beside the entry counts: a lockfile or manifest that names it (`package.json` with a
   lockfile for `node_modules`, `pyproject.toml` or `uv.lock` for `.venv`). The project profile may extend the list.
2. **A twin elsewhere:** a byte-identical file (sha256) outside the trees removed in this pass - in a tree that
   stays, in the main checkout, in a store the project declares (the profile names the feedback folder; session's
   call on the key name), in the archive, or as a blob git holds. A file with a twin can come back: tier 1.
3. **Identical copies across the trees being removed** (`spire3d-in/` in 23 trees): one copy is kept (in the archive
   when it has no twin outside), the rest go.
4. **No twin and not regenerable:** tier 2, moved to the archive; the five signals decide whether the person is told
   after or asked before.
5. **Paths a global or per-repository exclude matches** (`~/.config/git/ignore`, `.git/info/exclude`) are the
   person's local files (`.claude/settings.local.json`): tier 3, never regenerable (session's call).

Untracked render directories under names nothing declares (`main-25` holds 30 at the tree's root) are untracked
files: tier 3, whatever they look like.

## 7. The archive

The person accepted: before removing a tree, files with no copy anywhere are moved to `<project>-gc-archive/<tree>/`
beside the project's checkout (`twosuns-gc-archive/` in twosuns) - "only if it is not done quietly, and with an
approval". The person then refined it: small things are archived and the person is told; large ones are asked
first (section 3). Small things of one kind, or small things of one piece of work, go to the archive as one batch:
one archive entry, one line in the report, one restore (the person's decision, 2026-10-21).

- **Moved, not copied**, by rename on the same filesystem; across filesystems, copied, verified by hash, then
  deleted (session's call).
- **A manifest per move**: what, from which tree and path, sha256, size, when, which tier, who approved. Identical
  content is stored once and the manifest points to it.
- **One command restores**: `flotilla gc restore <tree>` (or one entry) puts the files back where they were, or into
  a fresh tree of the parked commit when the tree is gone (session's call on the command's name and shape).
- **Parked refs** belong to the archive entry of their tree.

**Lifetime (the person's decision):**

1. The archive lives one month.
2. A few days before the month ends (session's call: three), the orchestrator asks the person once for the whole
   archive: "in 3 days I remove 2.1 GB from the archive: 14 render series, list attached"; the person may keep a
   series longer or take something back into work.
3. No answer and the disk has room: the question moves to the next day.
4. Ignored three times: the archive stays another month, or until the disk runs out.
5. The disk runs out before the month is over: the question is raised at once.
6. No room at all: the person is told plainly to free space elsewhere or settle the archives, or work cannot go on
   (section 8).

## 8. A low disk

Session's call on the threshold: free space under 10 % of the volume or under 10 GB, whichever is larger; the
profile may change it. When it trips:

1. **Shrink first** - remove regenerable entries (`node_modules`, caches) from trees idle more than a day, even trees
   that stay (twosuns: about 9.6 GB of `node_modules` across 86 trees). Tier 1: it comes back with an install.
   "Idle" (session's call): no open row holds the tree for a live session, no process has its working directory in
   it, and no git activity in it (its index and HEAD) for 24 hours. A live session's tree is never touched, here or
   anywhere: on occasion b the session's finished branch is judged, its home tree is not (`switch` already needs it
   clean, and what is ignored in it belongs to the work going on).
2. **Raise the archive question early** (section 7, step 5).
3. **Stop starting work** - with no room left, the fleet starts no new session and the lane admits no new run until
   the person frees space or settles the archive. Live sessions are not stopped (session's call: "work cannot go on"
   read as "no new work starts").

## 9. Who speaks to the person

The person's decision: the person talks only to the orchestrator, or the session holding that role. The collector's
reports and questions go to the orchestrator as letters (the mechanism of `letters.py`: the command prints, the
session that ran it sends). If no orchestrator is live, the session that found the problem goes to the person.

- **Tier 1** acts, then reports in the orchestrator's next summary: counts by reason, the removal log, the restore
  command.
- **Tier 2, small**: the same, plus the archive list.
- **Tier 2, large, and tier 3**: a plan before any action; one question per series of like things (all renders of
  one tree, all untracked files of one tree), not one per file. Nothing in such a plan is done until the person
  answers; an unanswered question blocks only its own item, never a session's exit.
- **The person's answer is the authority, recorded, and only the collector acts on it** (session's call, following
  organize, decision 244, and decision 192: Claude's tool call never approves). A question is stored with an id;
  the person answers through the question door the way organize's answers are recorded, and the collector's own
  apply step carries out exactly the recorded answer. The manifest's "approved by" is that record. A session
  relaying "the person said yes" in a message approves nothing. A guard refuses sessions removing fleet trees or
  archive entries by hand (`git worktree remove --force`, `rm -rf` of a seat tree or of the archive), as the organize
  guard does for its moves - a guard that stops a mistaken session, not a determined one (decision 245).
- **Warnings with no action**: commits only on this machine ("`proto/teaser-v4`: 109 commits only on this disk"),
  a remote that lives on the same disk (twosuns' `origin` is `/home/max/workspace/twosuns-origin.git`), sole-holder
  trees.

## 10. What stays as it is

- Decisions 206 to 209 for branches, locks, the census and the seat-tree guard.
- `retire` and `fleet down` still judge the stopped seat's tree; they now apply sections 5 to 7.
- `flotilla fleet clean` stays the manual sweep, plan unless `--yes`, and becomes the daily pass.
- `leftovers.py` stops orphans in a tree before it is judged.

## 11. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals.** Finished sessions' trees and branches stop piling up; nothing the person or another AI made is lost
without the person's word; the person reads one report, not 91 "kept" lines.

**Non-goals.** Backup: the collector is not a copy of the project anywhere (twosuns has none off this disk; it is
named in the report, not fixed). Cleaning other machines' clones, the rig or the feedback folder itself.

**Anti-goals** (what would make it a failure even if it works):

1. **Silent loss of something that cannot come back** - an uncommitted script, the person's model, a commit no ref
   holds.
2. **A wrong "done"** - an orphan shell read as a live session (the tree stays forever), a dead session read as live,
   a live one read as dead, a local-only trunk read as safe.
3. **A collector so careful it is useless** - a daily list of 91 kept trees that nobody reads, and the person
   deleting by hand around it (it happened once already: 21.5 GB cleaned by hand in the feedback folder on
   2026-10-18).
4. **Archiving quietly** - the person's condition for the archive is that it is never quiet.

## 13. Left to the builder

- Where the collector lives in the code (an extension of `fleet/cleanup.py`, or a `gc` package beside it) and the
  CLI's shape beyond `flotilla gc restore`.
- The profile keys: declared stores, extra regenerable names, extra trunks, the size threshold, the disk threshold.
- How the daily pass is scheduled (the orchestrator's own loop or a cron the onboarding writes).
- Whether the rig's journal records where `--get` put its results; if it does, that record answers "made by flotilla
  on the rig" better than a name, and it should be used.
- How the orchestrator template (and its version) says what to do with the collector's letters.
